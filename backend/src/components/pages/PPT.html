01. What is Digital Forensics?

Digital Forensics is a scientific and systematic process used to identify, preserve, analyze, and present digital evidence in a legally acceptable manner. It plays a crucial role in cyber crime investigations, corporate security, and legal proceedings.

  • Identification: Detecting potential digital devices such as computers, mobile phones, servers, storage drives, and network logs.
  • Preservation: Protecting evidence from alteration using write blockers and maintaining the chain of custody.
  • Analysis: Examining data to recover deleted files, hidden information, logs, emails, and malware traces.
  • Presentation: Documenting findings clearly for courts, law enforcement agencies, or management.

Digital forensics ensures that electronic evidence remains authentic, reliable, and admissible in court.

03. Digital Forensic Tools

Digital forensic investigators rely on specialized tools to extract, analyze, and preserve digital evidence accurately.

EnCase

Commercial forensic suite used by law enforcement for disk imaging, file recovery, and reporting.

FTK Imager

Used to create forensic images and preview files without altering original evidence.

Wireshark

Network protocol analyzer used to inspect packets and detect suspicious network activity.

Autopsy

Open-source digital forensics platform with timeline analysis and keyword searching.

Volatility

Memory forensic tool used to analyze RAM for malware, running processes, and encryption keys.

Sleuth Kit

Command-line forensic tools for analyzing file systems and disk images.

06. Types of Cyber Crimes

  • Hacking: Gaining unauthorized access to systems to steal, modify, or destroy data.
  • Phishing: Fraudulent emails or websites designed to steal sensitive information such as passwords and OTPs.
  • Malware Attacks: Use of viruses, worms, trojans, and ransomware to damage systems.
  • Identity Theft: Stealing personal data for financial or criminal activities.
  • Cyber Stalking: Online harassment, monitoring, or threats using digital platforms.

Each cyber crime generates digital evidence that can be analyzed using forensic techniques.

07. Types of Digital Evidence

Digital evidence refers to any data stored or transmitted in digital form that can be used in investigations.

  • Hard disks, SSDs, USB drives
  • Mobile phones and SIM cards
  • Email communications and attachments
  • Browser history, cookies, cache
  • Network traffic logs and IP addresses

Proper handling of evidence is essential to maintain integrity and admissibility in court.

08. Email Header Analysis

Email headers contain hidden technical information that reveals the real sender of an email.

  • Sender IP address
  • Mail server routing path
  • Date and time stamps
  • SPF, DKIM, and DMARC authentication results

Email header analysis is widely used to investigate phishing, spoofing, and email fraud cases.

09. Mobile Device Forensics

Mobile devices store vast amounts of personal and sensitive data, making them critical sources of digital evidence.

  • Call logs and SMS messages
  • WhatsApp, Instagram, and social media chats
  • Deleted photos, videos, and documents
  • Location data and GPS history

Specialized tools are required to extract and analyze mobile data without damaging the device.

10. Network Forensics

Network forensics focuses on monitoring and analyzing network traffic to detect cyber attacks.

  • Packet capture analysis
  • IP address tracking
  • Firewall and router logs
  • Intrusion detection systems

It helps identify attackers, attack methods, and data breaches.

11. Legal & Ethical Aspects

  • Information Technology Act, 2000 (India)
  • Indian Evidence Act
  • User privacy and authorization
  • Chain of custody documentation

Digital forensic investigations must follow legal and ethical guidelines to ensure fairness and validity.

12. Case Study: Phishing Attack

A phishing email impersonating a bank caused financial fraud to a victim.

  • Email header analysis revealed sender IP
  • IP address traced to compromised server
